LULC / Land cover

Real or apparent coastal land-cover change?

Tildanga–Kamarkhola, Dacope, Khulna · 2018–2024

Research design
Research question

Which apparent land-cover transitions persist through time, which reverse, and which can be attributed to observation or classification instability?

Working hypothesis

Repeated, reference-supported observations may distinguish persistent change and genuine reversibility from unstable classification. This is a working hypothesis, not an established finding.

Proposed observations

Sentinel-210 m multispectral imagery; multi-date land-cover observations
Independent reference imageryVisual interpretation and validation where reliable coverage exists

Planned analytical workflow

  1. Define a candidate study area of approximately 100–200 km² and check the usable imagery record.
  2. Construct comparable satellite observations and candidate transition histories.
  3. Evaluate temporal persistence and reversibility with independent reference checks; reserve a later period for stability assessment.
  4. Report uncertainty by class and transition type, including cases that cannot be confidently resolved.
Feasibility constraints and decision gate

Uncertainty and risks

  • Clouds, shadows and seasonal water-level variation
  • Confusion among aquaculture, inundated cropland and other water surfaces
  • Limited independent reference coverage and uncertain historical labels

Go / no-go criterion

Proceed only if a defensible study boundary, comparable observations and independent reference support are available.

Next proposed output

Pilot coverage and reference-quality audit, followed by a validated transition-reliability assessment if feasible.

R01 / River dynamics

Persistent tidal-channel change and adjacent land-cover transitions

Shibsa–Bhadra–Kobadak candidate reaches; Rupsha–Pasur comparison · 2000–2025 (candidate epochs)

Pilot proposed
Research question

Do sustained tidal-channel shifts precede identifiable land-cover transitions in adjacent, previously terrestrial landscapes?

Working hypothesis

Persistent changes in mapped tidal-channel position may precede observable transitions in neighboring land cover. Temporal precedence alone cannot establish causation.

Proposed observations

Landsat Collection 2Candidate years 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020 and 2025
Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2Recent independent image checks where usable
CHIRPS and historical imageryRainfall context and reference interpretation

Planned analytical workflow

  1. Select provisional 8–12 km pilot reaches; verify locations, channel width and dry-season observation availability.
  2. Audit cloud-free coverage and compare recent optical and radar observations.
  3. If feasible, reconstruct persistent channel shifts and compare subsequent land-cover histories in adjacent previously terrestrial areas.
  4. Separate temporal ordering and co-occurrence from process attribution; report ambiguous observations.
Feasibility constraints and decision gate

Uncertainty and risks

  • Tidal-stage sensitivity and Landsat spatial resolution
  • Insufficient comparable image dates or narrow channels
  • Aquaculture, embankments and other competing explanations for land-cover transitions

Go / no-go criterion

Proceed only after the reach and imagery audit demonstrates sufficient spatial resolution, comparable coverage and defensible change detection.

Next proposed output

Verified pilot-reach inventory, satellite availability table and proceed/revise/stop decision.

F01 / Flood & drainage

Floodwater persistence and surface-drainage connectivity

Gutudia–Dumuria and Binerpota–Betna candidate areas, Satkhira–Dumuria · Pilot window: Aug–Nov 2024

Pilot proposed
Research question

Does observable surface-channel connectivity help explain post-event floodwater persistence in coastal floodplains?

Working hypothesis

Floodwater may persist differently in areas with different observable surface-drainage connectivity. Satellite observations cannot independently establish hydraulic controls.

Proposed observations

Sentinel-1 GRDCandidate acquisition audit: 1 August–30 November 2024
Sentinel-2, Landsat and JRC water historyPre-event surface-water context, aquaculture masking and optical interpretation
CHIRPS; GPM IMERG if necessaryRainfall-event timing and context

Planned analytical workflow

  1. Audit radar acquisitions by date, orbit, polarization, coverage and temporal gaps.
  2. Test whether observations resolve onset and recession; do not interpolate unobserved duration as fact.
  3. Check whether visible surface channels are sufficiently wide and continuous for a defensible connectivity indicator.
  4. Assess feasibility across events before estimating persistence or testing a relationship.
Feasibility constraints and decision gate

Uncertainty and risks

  • SAR revisit gaps, vegetation and aquaculture confusion
  • Unresolved narrow canals, culverts, embankments and sluices
  • No field-observed hydraulic calibration or validation data

Go / no-go criterion

Proceed only if event sampling, floodwater observability and resolvable drainage features support the analysis; otherwise revise or stop.

Next proposed output

Acquisition and rainfall-event audit, channel visibility assessment and explicit go/no-go decision.

03 / Evidence policy

What this research agenda commits to

Observations before explanations

Distinguish satellite-derived boundaries and classified patterns from physical processes. No inferred flood durations from missing acquisitions.

Open-data feasibility

Use publicly accessible satellite and secondary datasets. Do not assume the availability of field, hydraulic or proprietary calibration data. Candidate data must be audited first.

Transparent uncertainty

Document exclusions, unresolved cases and alternative explanations. Do not invent results, validation metrics or causal conclusions.
