Access to Social Services in Five Bangladeshi Cities

Why do vulnerable urban households struggle to access social protection even when they know a program exists? This mixed-methods study examined awareness, service delivery and inclusion in five secondary cities of Bangladesh.

Research question and objectives

The GIZ INTEGRATE initiative focuses on urban inclusion of internally displaced people and host communities. This assessment asked whether social protection and livelihood services were accessible in practice.

How do low-income households learn about, apply for and receive social services, what barriers disproportionately affect displaced residents and marginalized groups, and how could local delivery systems improve?

Objective 1

Map service awareness

Examine awareness and availability of DSS and other organizations' allowances, vocational training and microfinance.

Objective 2

Evaluate effective access

Investigate application, eligibility, selection, receipt and differences experienced by vulnerable groups.

Objective 3

Recommend improvements

Identify feasible ways to strengthen information, inclusion, transparency and interagency coordination.

Study area and research methods

The study brought together household survey evidence, community experience and institutional perspectives using a convergent parallel mixed-methods design.

Structured survey

Quantitative household interviews

Information on socioeconomic conditions, migration, awareness and access to services was gathered using KoboToolbox.

FGDs and KIIs

Qualitative field engagement

Community discussions and key-informant interviews explored lived experiences, institutional barriers and service delivery.

Desk review

Policy and organizational assessment

Social protection frameworks, government programs and service provision were examined alongside primary findings.

The report describes an initial target of 400, a revised target of 500 and 514 completed household interviews; city sample sizes were uneven.

Finding 1 — Awareness did not guarantee access

Many residents knew that social allowance programs existed. Knowing how to apply, satisfying documentation requirements and receiving assistance were separate challenges.

Aware of allowances

51%–82%

This range describes awareness across the five study cities.

Applications in Khulna

As low as 22%

The report identifies a low application rate despite awareness; approval levels also varied by program.

Barriers described in the report

Confusion about documents and application procedures, eligibility tied to residence or origin, reliance on intermediaries, and uneven beneficiary selection weakened access.

Finding 2 — Skills training and microfinance had limited reach

Livelihood-oriented services were less visible than allowance schemes, while limited outreach, unsuitable facilities and restrictive access criteria compounded the problem.

Skills development

10.74%–27.59% aware

DSS training awareness was low in each city; actual participation was often lower still.

DSS microfinance

Under 7% accessed

The report's executive summary describes very limited reach of microfinance among surveyed households.

Finding 3 — Exclusion was not experienced equally

Displacement, gender and disability could compound difficulties navigating registration and institutional service systems.

Climate-displaced residents

Documentation barriers

Proof of residence, origin-linked requirements and incomplete documentation could prevent eligible people from accessing services.

Women

Care and scheduling constraints

Rigid training times, limited childcare and programs not designed for women's needs reduced participation.

Persons with disabilities

Physical and program barriers

Inaccessible training settings, limited adapted programs and exclusionary processes restricted equitable access.

Institutional bottlenecks

The study also described fragmented coordination among service providers, dependence on informal communication channels and uneven digital literacy.

Recommendations for inclusive services

The team proposed reforms to how social services are administered, communicated and delivered, rather than simply expanding program lists.

Recommendations describe proposed reforms. The study did not evaluate implemented outcomes from these proposals.

Interpretation and limitations

The findings illuminate delivery gaps within selected urban low-income communities, but the work was cross-sectional, depended partly on self-reported experiences, and used uneven samples across cities.

Supported conclusion

Access requires more than awareness

The quantitative and qualitative evidence identifies documentation, information, accessibility and coordination problems.

Not established

National rates or reform effects

These findings should not be extrapolated to all of Bangladesh or treated as causal proof of how any intervention would perform.

Why this work matters

A service cannot protect vulnerable households if eligibility and application remain difficult to navigate. Findings from five cities highlight how inclusive documentation, accessible training, clear communication and better coordination can help social protection reach those it is intended to serve.
