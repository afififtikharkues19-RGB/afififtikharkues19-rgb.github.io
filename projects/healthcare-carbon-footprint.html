Healthcare Carbon Footprint Assessment

How much greenhouse gas did nine hospitals generate, which activities contributed most, and where could healthcare facilities prioritize practical emissions reductions?

An institutional assessment initiated by IEDCR with technical assistance from WHO, with Geo Smart Planning project involvement.

Research question and objectives

Healthcare facilities provide essential care but also generate emissions through energy use, transport, medical equipment and services. Establishing a facility-level baseline helps distinguish the most important contributors and identify opportunities for reduction.

How do greenhouse gas emissions and their sources vary across different types of healthcare facilities in Bangladesh, and what monitoring and mitigation measures could address them?

Objective 1

Identify emission sources

Account for direct and indirect sources, including relevant supply-chain categories, at the assessed hospitals.

Objective 2

Compare facility footprints

Examine measured emissions at three healthcare levels and identify important operational contributors.

Objective 3

Track energy consumption

Develop an approach for monitoring and projecting energy use through structured records and a dashboard.

Objective 4

Design mitigation plans

Propose facility-specific options for a more sustainable, lower-carbon healthcare system.

Study design and methods

The study included three tertiary hospitals, three district hospitals and three upazila health complexes. Emissions were calculated using the Aga Khan Development Network Carbon Management Tool, with records collected from hospital departments.

Study coverage

Nine hospitals

Three facilities at each service tier, selected across different regions of Bangladesh.

Data collection

Multiple operational sources

Electricity, fuel, transport, anaesthetic gases, refrigerants, medical waste and procurement records where available.

Analysis

Comparable CO₂e estimates

Three-month facility totals were assembled and compared. Annual footprints and mitigation benefits were projected separately.

Reporting boundary: July–September 2023 was the measured period. This nine-facility sample is not a measurement of the national healthcare system's total annual emissions.

Key finding: facility footprints varied substantially

The largest three-month footprints occurred at the tertiary hospitals. Absolute totals reflect different facility sizes, patient loads, service requirements and reporting circumstances; they should not be read as efficiency rankings.

Recorded footprints ranged from 18.28 tCO₂e at Saidpur Upazila Health Complex to 2,688.64 tCO₂e at Sylhet MAG Osmani Medical College Hospital during the same three-month period.

Tertiary care

1,663–2,689 tCO₂e

Sylhet MAG Osmani reported the largest total, followed by Mymensingh and Kurmitola.

District hospitals

171–313 tCO₂e

Sirajganj had the highest of the three district totals; Satkhira the lowest.

Upazila complexes

18–94 tCO₂e

These smaller facilities also varied widely, from Saidpur to Gournadi.

Where did the emissions come from?

There was no single dominant source across all hospitals. For example, purchased electricity dominated Kurmitola's operating profile, while medical gases and waste were more important at Sylhet MAG Osmani. The report also identified supply-chain activities as important contributors to full carbon footprints.

How to read the source chart: The report's hospital-level source-composition figures exclude supply-chain emissions, whereas the headline facility totals cover a wider accounting boundary. We preserve that distinction rather than combining incompatible percentages.

Monitoring and mitigation recommendations

The project assessed potential interventions and piloted energy monitoring. Recommendations included more reliable recordkeeping, energy efficiency, solar opportunities, safer waste-management systems and possible carbon-offset pathways. These were proposals and projections, not reductions verified across all hospitals.

Digital records and metering

Track energy demand and other resources consistently to identify hotspots and evaluate future performance.

Energy and renewable options

Improve lighting and equipment practices and assess suitable solar generation opportunities.

Waste segregation and management

Consider waste-reduction opportunities, keeping hazardous medical waste separate from any non-hazardous material eligible for composting.

Long-term sequestration and offsets

Evaluate proposed planting or carbon-market scenarios carefully and verify real outcomes before reporting neutrality.

Interpretation and limitations

The work supplies a useful baseline for the nine facilities, but important data and scope limitations affect any larger claims.

Three-month measurement window

Annual totals were extrapolated rather than recorded for a complete year.

Incomplete records

Some waste, water and travel values came from estimates or dispersed handwritten documentation rather than consistent digital measurements.

Limited national inference

Nine hospitals cannot establish a nationally representative carbon inventory or hospital-level emissions intensity by themselves.

Projected benefits

Mitigation and carbon-neutral scenarios require implementation, ongoing measurements and independent verification.

Institutional context

IEDCR, WHO and project partners

The study was initiated by Bangladesh's Institute of Epidemiology, Disease Control and Research with technical assistance from the World Health Organization. The report lists a separate assessment team and technical workgroup.

Why this work matters

Sound emissions accounting is the starting point for practical, measurable decarbonization. This study shows why facility-level evidence, complete records and repeated monitoring are needed before healthcare institutions can reliably claim reductions.
