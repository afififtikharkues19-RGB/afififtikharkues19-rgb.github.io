BSc dissertation · Mongla, Bangladesh · 1993–2023

Ecosystem Service Accounting in Mongla Upazila

Land-cover change, ecosystem valuation and resource-based services

An assessment of how land-cover transitions relate to the estimated contributions of forests, rivers, aquaculture, agriculture and settlements to the coastal landscape's ecosystem services.

30 years1993–2023
4 epochsLandsat-based land-cover maps
5 classesland-cover categories
2023resource and tourism accounts

Research question

How did land cover change in Mongla Upazila between 1993 and 2023, and what do those changes imply for the estimated value and recorded supply of ecosystem services?

The study links satellite-derived land-cover extent with monetary valuation coefficients, while examining reported production and revenue from selected provisioning and recreational services.

Research objectives

01

Identify and compare land-use and land-cover patterns in Mongla Upazila over four observation years.

02

Assess ecosystem-service trade-offs associated with different land-cover categories.

03

Estimate ecosystem-service values and describe the physical supply and reported revenue of selected services.

Study area

Mongla Upazila, in coastal Bagerhat District, includes parts of the Sundarbans mangrove landscape, major rivers, aquaculture areas, agricultural land and settlements. The mixture of natural ecosystems and human land uses makes the area suitable for an ecosystem-accounting comparison.

Data and methodology

The research combined remote sensing and GIS with coefficient-based ecosystem valuation and reported service-production records.

01

Land-cover mapping

Dry-season 30 m Landsat imagery from 1993, 2003, 2013 and 2023 was classified into forest, river, agriculture, aquaculture, and settlements/other land using supervised maximum-likelihood classification and GIS mapping.

02

Ecosystem valuation

Mapped land-cover areas were multiplied by published per-hectare biome-value coefficients to estimate annual ecosystem-service values. A ±50% coefficient sensitivity assessment examined the dependence of results on valuation assumptions.

03

Service accounts

Secondary records from government sources supported physical production and reported revenue accounts for fish, crops, selected forest products and recreational tourism.

The modelled annual ecosystem-service values are coefficient-based estimates, not observed market revenue. The service-revenue figures are a separate account. Classification validation was available for 2003–2023, not for the 1993 baseline.

Key findings

The results are presented in two related parts: mapped landscape change followed by estimates of ecosystem contributions and recorded services.

Land-cover transitions

Forest remained dominant as other land uses expanded

Forest occupied approximately 82% of the mapped area in 1993, compared with 69% in 2023. Over the same period, aquaculture rose from a negligible mapped share to about 5%, while settlements and other lands increased from approximately 5% to 12%.

How to interpret the change

The maps describe shifts among broad classified land-cover categories. They do not, by themselves, establish the causes of forest loss or prove that every inland waterbody was actively used for aquaculture.

Ecosystem-service valuation

The coefficient-based value estimate declined across the study period

The estimated annual ecosystem-service value decreased from 253.76 × 10⁸ US$ in 1993 to 212.94 × 10⁸ US$ in 2023, using the study's transferred biome-value coefficients. Forest accounted for the vast majority of the 2023 estimate, at 208.78 × 10⁸ US$.

Forest dominated the modelled values because of both its mapped area and the high assigned per-hectare mangrove coefficient. In the sensitivity assessment, changing the forest coefficient produced much greater shifts in total ESV than changing coefficients for the other land-cover classes.

Important distinction

These are valuation-model outputs, not measured cash flow or verified contributions to local GDP. The imported value coefficients and the broad land-cover classifications are important sources of uncertainty.

Provisioning and recreational services

Recorded production and tourism provide a different economic perspective

For 2023, the collected records reported approximately US$78.25 million in fish-production revenue and US$8.17 million from agricultural production. Forest-product receipts and tourism were also recorded; the Chandpai tourism account reported 13,007 visitors and approximately US$43,563 in revenue.

These are reported sectoral revenues for specific activities and administrative sources; they must not be added directly to the transferred-coefficient ecosystem-service estimates.

Interpretation and implications

The research illustrates how changing land-cover composition can alter coefficient-based estimates of ecosystem-service provision. Mangrove-associated forest remains central to the modelled accounts, while the recorded data demonstrate the practical roles of fisheries, agriculture, forest-resource use and tourism.

Research contribution

By combining multi-epoch land-cover maps, monetary valuation assumptions, sensitivity tests and selected production records, the study provides a spatially explicit account of ecosystem services in a coastal district where conservation and economic activities overlap.

Limitations

  • Model assumptions: ESV calculations use externally published biome coefficients rather than locally estimated willingness-to-pay or a complete regional environmental-economic account.
  • Mapping uncertainty: the 1993 baseline was not independently validated; later classification accuracy assessments covered only the available reference categories.
  • Class definitions: aquaculture was identified using inland-water spectral patterns and may not capture all aquaculture systems or distinguish every alternative use.
  • Different economic measures: estimated annual ecosystem-service values and recorded government or market revenues describe different concepts and should not be compared as equivalent quantities.

Research overview

Ecosystem Service Accounting of Mongla Upazilla of Bagerhat District in Bangladesh. BSc dissertation, Environmental Science, Khulna University (2023). The study connects land-cover change with the estimated and recorded services of coastal ecosystems.
