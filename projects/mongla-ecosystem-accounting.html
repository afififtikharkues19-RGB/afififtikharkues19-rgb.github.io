BSc dissertation · Khulna University · 2023

Ecosystem-service accounting in coastal Mongla

How did Mongla’s land cover change over three decades—and what can those changes, together with resource and tourism records, tell us about the services its ecosystems provide?

Dissertation title: Ecosystem Service Accounting of Mongla Upazilla of Bagerhat District in Bangladesh

The investigation

Research question & objectives

Land-cover maps show where ecosystems are located. Ecosystem-service estimates attach values to those mapped areas. Resource records show a different kind of evidence: quantities collected and revenues recorded. This study examined all three.

How did land-use and land-cover patterns in Mongla change between 1993 and 2023, and what do these changes imply for estimated ecosystem-service values and recorded provisioning and recreational benefits?

Objective one

Map ecosystem extent

Identify and compare land-cover patterns across four satellite observation years.

Objective two

Assess trade-offs

Examine how changing areas of forests, rivers, agriculture, aquaculture and settlements affect coefficient-based ecosystem-service estimates.

Objective three

Account for services

Estimate land-cover-based service values and examine separately recorded production and tourism revenues.

Where the work took place

Mongla: a coastal landscape shaped by the Sundarbans

Mongla Upazila lies in southwestern Bangladesh near the Sundarbans mangrove forest and the Pasur river system. The mapped landscape includes extensive forest, river channels, agricultural land, aquaculture areas, and settlements linked to the port and surrounding communities.

How the work was done

Data & analytical approach

The research combined satellite classification with two different forms of ecosystem-service assessment. Keeping those methods separate is important because their monetary results do not measure the same thing.

Spatial evidence

Classify land cover

Dry-season Landsat imagery at 30 m resolution for 1993, 2003, 2013 and 2023; supervised maximum-likelihood classification into five classes; GIS maps and area comparisons. Google Earth reference imagery supported accuracy assessments for 2003, 2013 and 2023.

Model-based estimates

Value mapped ecosystems

Combine mapped class areas with published annual ecosystem-service value coefficients. Because no corresponding biome was available for aquaculture, use a proxy for its food-production contribution. Test how changing each coefficient by ±50% affects total estimated value.

Observed accounts

Review resource records

Compile available administrative production and revenue information for timber, thatching materials, fuelwood, honey and wax, fish, agriculture and tourism. These values describe specific economic activities, not the full value of ecosystem services.

Two distinct monetary measures: “Estimated ecosystem-service value” is a coefficient-based approximation (mapped area × published value per hectare). “Recorded revenue” is an observed or administratively reported monetary amount for specific resources and services. They must not be added together or treated as directly interchangeable.

Results · Landscape change

A forest-dominated landscape with shifting land uses

Across four classified dates, forest occupied most of Mongla, but its mapped share decreased. Aquaculture and the combined settlements-and-others class expanded; agriculture rose earlier in the record before declining by 2023.

Results · Modeled service values

Estimated ecosystem-service value declined

Land-cover area was multiplied by published value coefficients to estimate annual ecosystem-service values. The resulting series decreased between 1993 and 2023, largely because forest carried a high value coefficient and occupied less mapped area at the end of the period.

Why the forest coefficient matters

The forest category dominated the calculated total. In 2023 it represented approximately 69% of mapped area but contributed 208.78 of the 212.94 units shown in the valuation account. The analysis also varied the value coefficients by ±50% to test the sensitivity of the total.

Sensitivity test

Forest was the main driver

The forest coefficient had the largest sensitivity value (approximately 0.93–0.95), showing how strongly the total depends on the assumed mangrove-service valuation.

Interpretation

Estimates, not measured losses

A decrease in this account does not measure lost income, ecological function or welfare directly. It reflects mapped land-cover differences under an assumed set of values per hectare.

Accounting note: the values are derived from land-cover areas and published coefficients rather than observed transactions. The changes should be understood as indicative comparisons, not precise market valuations.

Results · Resource and tourism accounts

Recorded production and revenues tell a different story

The thesis also assembled accounts for specific products and activities. These records show the economic importance of fisheries, agriculture, forest products and visits to the Chandpai range, without claiming to capture their total ecosystem-service value.

Provisioning services

Fisheries and agriculture

Fish and agricultural production were large contributors to the specific production-and-revenue records assembled for 2023.

Forest and cultural services

Products and tourism

The Chandpai accounts included timber, thatching materials, fuelwood, honey and wax, and revenue from visitors. The coverage and measurement basis differ from the land-cover valuation model.

Synthesis

What this research establishes—and what remains uncertain

Placing mapped extent, modeled values and resource-use accounts side by side offers a richer view than any one of these measures alone.

Spatial transformation

Four Landsat classifications document the changing balance of forest, agriculture, aquaculture, rivers and settlements/other land uses.

Valuation dependence

Estimated annual service values fell under the chosen coefficients, with forest area and the high forest coefficient driving the result.

Economic activity

Production and tourism records provide a separate indication of resource-linked economic activity, not a replacement for ecological valuation.

The boundaries of the evidence

Directly supported

What was observed or calculated

  • Mapped five-category land cover for four dates.
  • Land-cover class area and proportion changes.
  • Service-value estimates under published coefficients and sensitivity adjustments.
  • Selected production, resource and tourism records.
Not established

What the study cannot infer

  • Precise ecosystem condition or the full value of nature to local communities.
  • A one-to-one correspondence between mapped aquaculture and all active farms.
  • A causal explanation of the observed class transitions.
  • Independent map accuracy for 1993 or locally validated service-value coefficients.

Important limitations: the 1993 classification could not be independently validated; overall accuracy was reported for 2003 (87.48%), 2013 (88.63%) and 2023 (88.61%). Dry-season imagery, class definitions and the use of nonlocal proxy coefficients introduce additional uncertainty. The coefficient sensitivity test explores one part of that uncertainty rather than eliminating it.

Research significance

Linking landscape mapping with the value of ecosystem services

This dissertation connected three forms of evidence—satellite-derived ecosystem extent, coefficient-based service estimates and records of resource use—to examine environmental change in Mongla. Its most important methodological lesson is the need to distinguish what is observed from what is assumed when translating land-cover patterns into monetary values.
