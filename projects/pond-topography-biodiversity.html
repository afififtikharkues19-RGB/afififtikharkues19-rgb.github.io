Purpose and objectives

Sadhu Francis Para Pond is used by nearby residents for daily activities and has longstanding community importance. Bank erosion, maintenance gaps and vegetation loss prompted a baseline survey to inform possible rehabilitation. The work focused on the evidence required for planning rather than declaring restoration success.

What are the pond’s present physical, hydrological and ecological conditions, and what baseline information is needed to plan its conservation and revitalization?

Objective 01

Map the pond and drainage setting

Survey surface elevations, pond boundaries, adjacent open spaces and drainage slopes to support topographic and contour mapping.

Objective 02

Characterize the water and soil environment

Document groundwater and salinity conditions, soil structure, infiltration, permeability and water quality using field and laboratory methods.

Objective 03

Assess ecological and runoff context

Record existing trees and physical features while examining rainfall, runoff paths and potential water availability around the pond.

Intended use

Inform a conservation plan

Bring the field evidence together to help assess pond-bank stabilization, water management and ecologically sensitive revitalization options.

Study design and methodology

The project combined geodetic surveying with hydrological, soil and water investigations. The methods were intended to produce a consistent spatial baseline and characterize how the pond interacts with its immediate surroundings.

01 · Terrain and geometry

RTK and Total Station

Survey control was tied to a Survey of Bangladesh benchmark, with temporary benchmarks used for instrument setup. Elevation points and nearby built features formed the basis of a topographic surface and pond profile.

02 · Soil and hydrology

Groundwater and infiltration

Manual boreholes, soil cores and double-ring infiltrometer tests were described for the pond’s four corners and a nearby open-space location, supporting assessment of soil and subsurface conditions.

03 · Water and ecology

Water chemistry and site features

Water samples were taken from three points to assess ten listed physicochemical parameters. Existing vegetation, drainage features and site conditions were included in the field assessment.

What the survey documents

The report specifies a multi-part evidence base for pond rehabilitation. The categories below describe the survey activities and analytical outputs, not independently verified improvements in pond condition.

Topography and drainage

Relief, slopes and site features

Elevation observations, a longitudinal profile, surrounding roads and drainage features were gathered for digital terrain modelling and interpretation of water movement.

Ground and surface water

Hydrological conditions

Groundwater depth and saline-water observations were included alongside rainfall and runoff analysis using Bangladesh Meteorological Department data and GIS.

Soil properties

Infiltration and permeability

Field infiltration tests and undisturbed samples were intended to characterize soil texture, porosity and hydraulic conductivity around the pond.

Water chemistry and ecology

Water quality and biodiversity context

The programme covered pH, dissolved oxygen, BOD, COD, TDS, turbidity, chloride, suspended solids, total alkalinity and conductivity, as well as surrounding vegetation and habitat context.

Evidence boundary

The document supplied for this website contains the introduction and methods, but does not include numerical water-quality results, measured soil properties, biodiversity totals or final mapped restoration outcomes. Those results are therefore not claimed here.

How the assessment can inform restoration

Where fully validated measurements are available, the combined baseline can help the project team evaluate conservation options more systematically.

Protect pond edges

Use elevation and drainage information to identify areas where bank condition and surface runoff need closer attention before selecting interventions.

Manage water and soil

Bring groundwater, soil permeability, infiltration and water-quality results into a single framework for assessing water-management options.

Support community and habitat value

Consider existing trees, public uses and local environmental conditions while designing improvements to the pond and its surroundings.

Interpretation: This is a baseline-oriented applied survey. The project documentation does not establish that bank erosion has been reduced, water quality has improved, or biodiversity has increased following interventions.

Why this work matters

Reliable restoration planning begins with a clear account of the existing site: its terrain, water pathways, soil properties and ecological setting. This project illustrates how conventional field surveying and environmental measurements can be combined to create an evidence base for community pond conservation.
