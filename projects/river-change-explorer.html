Mapped erosion and accretion by interval

Six consecutive five-year intervals summarize mapped bankline-water-boundary change. Gross erosion values were retained from the original mapping; accretion totals were subsequently reconciled with the measured channel-area change.

Read the balance carefully: gross mapped erosion totals 36.06 km²; closure-reconciled accretion totals 23.44 km². Their difference equals the 12.62 km² increase in mapped channel area across 1994–2024. These are descriptive water-boundary-derived measurements, not tide-normalized physical bank-erosion rates.

What this evidence can—and cannot—show

Satellite observations reveal spatial and temporal patterns, but both mapping conditions and analytical definitions matter when interpreting environmental change.

Water boundary ≠ bank position

Tidal stage, image resolution and extraction decisions can shift an apparent waterline. A mapped difference is not automatically physical erosion or a tide-corrected bank migration measurement.

Early classifications are less certain

The 1994 and 1999 land-cover maps could not be independently validated. Overall accuracy for the 2004–2024 classifications was approximately 76.7–79.8%.

Land cover ≠ causal mechanism

A greater mapped waterbody extent, declining cropland and growing urban land document classification patterns. They do not by themselves establish why those transitions occurred.

From observation to explanation

The visual record documents substantial mapped changes along the river corridor. Understanding which changes reflect physical river processes—and why different reaches respond differently—requires hydrodynamic evidence, sediment-transport analysis and careful treatment of observation uncertainty.
