Land-Use Transitions and River Morphodynamics

The Rupsha–Pasur Corridor, Bangladesh, 1994–2024

Three decades of mapped river-channel change, land-cover transition and household-scale vulnerability.

+12.62 km²mapped channel-area increase
200households surveyed
246transects in dissertation analysis

01 / Central result

What the evidence shows

Between 1994 and 2024, mapped channel area increased from 62.84 km² to 75.46 km². Reported gross mapped erosion was 36.06 km² and closure-reconciled accretion was 23.44 km².

02 / Findings

Key findings

  1. Average mapped channel width increased from 486.4 m to 581.3 m across the study period.
  2. Cropland declined from 18.5% to 9.2% of the study area; mapped waterbody increased from 10.2% to 20.1%.
  3. A 200-household assessment examined socioeconomic conditions and livelihood vulnerability (composite LVI 0.443; LVI-IPCC contributing-factor score −0.054).

04 / Research methods

Approach & tools

Multi-temporal Landsat imagery, MNDWI-derived water boundaries, erosion–accretion mapping and LULC classification; household surveys; LVI/LVI-IPCC and regression analysis.

05 / Interpretation

Limitations & responsible attribution

Mapped water–land boundaries are proxies affected by image resolution and tidal stage; land-cover change alone does not establish its causal drivers. The accretion values were reconciled numerically to close against the channel-area series.

Reference: MSc dissertation: Analyzing Land-Use Transitions and Socio-Economic Conditions in Relation to Morphological Change of the Rupsha and Pasur Rivers in Bangladesh.
